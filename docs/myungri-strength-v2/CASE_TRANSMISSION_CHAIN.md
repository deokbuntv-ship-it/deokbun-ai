# CASE TRANSMISSION CHAINS — S1.6

Status: **S1.6 RESEARCH ARTIFACT — NOT CANONICAL DOCTRINE**

Purpose: distinguish **independent reasoning** from **copied tradition** by
tracing, chart by chart, who transmitted a case to whom. A chart with five
citations and one argument is not five bridges — it is one argument, cited
five times. This file exists so nobody re-counts it as five.

Fields per §15: `CHART_CASE_ID` · `EARLIEST_KNOWN_SOURCE` · `TRANSMISSION_N`
· `COMMENTARY_ADDED?` · `JUDGMENT_CHANGED?` · `TERMINOLOGY_CHANGED?` ·
`SOURCE_DEPENDENCE`.

## What these six chains establish about transmission generally

1. **A chart's transmission history and its judgment history are separate
   questions**, and conflating them is the single most common way a false
   bridge gets manufactured. CHAIN-01 and CHAIN-06 show borrowed charts with
   real disagreement; CHAIN-02 shows a borrowed chart with **no** disagreement
   across three re-derivations — both are informative, and neither should be
   read as the other.
2. **Chronology, read from a source's own dateline, is the most reliable
   independence test found in this program** (CHAIN-03) — more reliable than
   any argument from vocabulary or from citation absence, both of which can be
   circumstantial.
3. **A single classical author can hold multiple incompatible judgments of one
   chart within one book** (CHAIN-01). Before crediting any two-author
   "disagreement," the tradition's own internal consistency must be checked
   first — this is the specific failure mode `BRIDGE_SEARCH_REGISTER.md` §10
   documents as the column-split-table trap.
